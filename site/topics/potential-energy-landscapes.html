MEC12 · Motion & forces

Potential-energy landscapes

Put a marble in a bowl and it ends up at the bottom, whatever you do. Balance it on top of an upturned bowl and it rolls off at the first puff of air. The same picture, a landscape of stored energy, explains why diamonds last for ever and how a chatbot learns.

Reading time ≈ 10 minSimulation: Moderate (M)Video: 8–12 min

The 30-second version

Draw stored energy as the height of a landscape. Then the force on a ball is always downhill, as strong as the slope is steep. Valleys are stable resting places, hilltops are unstable, and a hill between two valleys is a barrier that the ball can only cross if it has enough energy. One picture covers a huge range of physics.

0 · The story

Take a salad bowl and drop a marble in. Wherever you release it, it rolls down, overshoots, rolls back, and gradually settles at the bottom. Try again from a different spot: the same ending. The bottom is a place the marble always returns to.

Now turn the bowl upside down and balance the marble on its very top. If you are patient, you can get it to sit for a moment. But the slightest breath, a vibration of the table, and it rolls away and never returns.

Both are positions where the marble feels no push at all. Both are “equilibrium.” Yet they could not be more different. What decides? Not any force you can see. It is the shape of the landscape the marble sits on.

1 · The illusion

“Every force needs its own rule”

At school forces arrive one by one, each with its own formula: gravity, springs, electricity, friction. They seem unrelated, and for each one you must learn the rule for how strong it is and which way it points.

There is a unifying picture. For many forces, you can store energy in the arrangement of things: height in a gravity field, stretch in a spring, separation of charges. If you plot that stored energy against position, the force at each place is simply the slope. Learn to read a landscape and you have learned them all.

2 · Where the idea came from

  1. 1788Joseph-Louis Lagrange, in Mécanique analytique, shows that a mechanical system is in stable equilibrium where its potential energy is at a minimum.
  2. 1846Peter Dirichlet gives the first rigorous proof of that stability statement.
  3. 1853William Rankine introduces the term potential energy for energy stored by position.
  4. 1889Svante Arrhenius shows that how fast a chemical reaction goes depends on the height of an energy barrier the molecules must climb.
  5. 1969Cyrus Levinthal points out that proteins find their folded shape far too quickly to be searching at random: they must be rolling down a landscape.
  6. 2020DeepMind’s AlphaFold 2 predicts protein shapes from their sequence with near-laboratory accuracy at the CASP14 competition.

3 · The model

4 · The reveal

Slope is force

Think of the energy curve as the real shape of a landscape, with gravity pulling the ball down it. A steep slope accelerates the ball strongly. A flat stretch exerts no force. Because the picture has the same shape whatever the underlying cause, it works as well for a mass on a spring (a bowl), electrons near a nucleus, or two atoms bonded together.

The total energy line is the second key. A ball released at rest from some height can never rise above where it started, just as with the roller coaster. The region where the curve is below that line is where the ball may roam; where the curve rises above it, the ball can’t go. The two edges are the points where it turns round.

Valleys, hills and barriers

A valley is a stable place: a small push and it rolls back. A hilltop is unstable: a small push sends it away for good. A barrier is a hill between two valleys: a ball in one valley can only reach the other if it has more energy than the barrier is high. With friction draining energy, a ball ends up in whichever valley it was nearest to, and stays there, which is how a double well makes a memory: a switch that remembers which side it was last pushed to.

The surprise

The same picture explains why diamond lasts for ever, even though graphite has lower energy. Diamond sits in a shallow valley behind a huge barrier. It would turn to graphite if it could climb out, but at room temperature nothing ever does.

5 · Where it shows up

Happening right now

Right now inside every cell of your body, enzymes are lowering energy barriers so that reactions that would take centuries finish in milliseconds at body temperature. Training a neural network, such as the one behind a chatbot, is a ball rolling down a vast, many-dimensional landscape of “error,” step by step, looking for a low valley. And in your phone’s flash memory, bits are stored as electric charge trapped behind a barrier in a landscape much like this one.

  • Chemistry and biologyReaction rates depend on barrier heights; enzymes and catalysts lower them.
  • Protein foldingA chain of amino acids rolls down a funnel-shaped landscape to its working shape.
  • Machine learningTraining is a descent on a landscape of error, called gradient descent.
  • Memory and switchesTwo valleys separated by a barrier store one bit.
  • Structural stabilityA structure is safe where it sits in a valley and unsafe on a hilltop.
  • MaterialsDiamond, glass and many alloys sit in valleys that are not the lowest, held there by barriers.

6 · Where the simulation lies

Honest limits

This is a one-dimensional landscape: a ball sliding on a curve, not rolling, and with no spin. Real systems have many dimensions. The friction here is a simple drag proportional to speed. The heights are drawn in pixels and the shapes are made up to show the ideas. Everything is classical, so the ball can never slip through a barrier it doesn’t have the energy to climb (quantum particles can, which is a later chapter). Real molecules also get random thermal kicks, which a single button only roughly imitates.

Remember

Three things to take away

  1. Force is the slope. Always downhill in stored energy, strongest where steepest.
  2. Valleys are stable, hilltops are not. Barriers separate one stable state from another.
  3. Total energy sets the limit. The ball can only go where the landscape is below its energy.

Glossary and sources

Potential energy
Energy stored in the arrangement of a system, as a function of position.
Equilibrium
A position where the net force is zero.
Stable / unstable
Whether a small nudge is followed by a return or by running away.
Activation barrier
The energy needed to get from one state to another over a hill.
